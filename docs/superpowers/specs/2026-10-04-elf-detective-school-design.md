# NBCC Elf Detective School

Date: 2026-10-04. The idea is the head trustee's. Jaimie answered every question below one at a time
on 2 to 4 October and signed the plan off on 3 October ("Signed off as it is"). The plan page Jaimie
signed off, with the sketches, is a private page: its link is in Jaimie's own notes, not here.

The point, in Jaimie's words: a new way to raise money once the Festive Ball is over, aimed at
children but open to everyone, fun and exciting, which also tells families who have never heard of
NBCC who we are. All the admin happens through the website; the team only posts on social media, and
the website tells them what to post and when.

## The game in one paragraph

24 cheeky elves have escaped from the Elves' Workshop in Annbank. A grown-up enrols each detective at
NBCC Elf Detective School. Each day from 1 to 24 December one elf hides in a local business. A clue
comes out, families lock in a guess, and the first detective to reach the business and read the
secret word on the elf wins it. Everyone else can still visit to collect a stamp. Doors add up to
promotions, new clothes for their elf, graduation, and for those who visit all 24 places, a chance at
the grand prize, delivered on Christmas Day. The deadline is the Night Before Christmas.

## Dates

- Tuesday 1 to Thursday 24 December 2026: one door a day. Weekends are 5/6, 12/13 and 19/20.
- Christmas Eve: graduation and judging. Christmas Day (Friday): the grand prize is delivered.

## Decisions (Jaimie's, in the order they were made)

1. **Name:** NBCC Elf Detective School, Class of 2026. The story is the runaway elves. Never use
   "Elf on the Shelf" (a registered trademark) in any public wording; say "your naughtiest elf".
2. **Places:** anywhere in NBCC's area, any day. Jaimie knows this favours whoever lives nearest and
   that visiting all 24 means a lot of driving, so far-away children must have something to do daily.
3. **Guessing:** each family locks in one guess a day from the public list of this year's hiding
   places (the list is public from launch, without the days). They can change it until the elf is
   found. A right guess puts a magnifying glass on that door. Late joiners can only complete past
   doors by visiting, because the answer is already on the door. The list shrinks as elves are
   found.
4. **Race start:** there is no "elf wakes up" time. The race starts the moment the clue comes out.
   The clue time is the fairness lever: after school and work on school days, in the morning at
   weekends and in the school holidays. Each day's time is set in the admin. Weekday places must be
   open into the evening; the admin warns when a place closes soon after its clue.
5. **Door opening:** the door opens fully the moment the elf is found, showing the place and "Found
   by Detective R at 5:47pm", and guessing closes. Every door shows its clue time with a countdown,
   and the clue appears on the website at the same second for everyone; the email follows.
6. **Claiming:** each elf wears a tag with a secret word. The finder scans the business's QR poster
   and types the word. The website decides who was first and shows a gold "You're first!" screen for
   the staff, who hand over the elf and prize. The business puts the elf out at clue time and gets
   an automatic reminder that day.
7. **Stamps:** visiting a place after its door has opened earns a candy cane stamp. Scanning the
   poster asks which detectives are here, then checks the phone's location once (nothing is kept).
   Anyone who will not share location takes a photo at the place instead. Someone who has not signed
   up and scans a poster gets a "Join Elf Detective School" page.
8. **Ranks and finishes:** a door is done when it is cracked or visited. Trainee at sign-up, Junior
   Detective at 6 doors, Detective at 12, Senior Detective at 18. **Graduate** = all 24 doors done:
   a certificate and a link to the police officer's video. **Gold seal** = all 24 places visited:
   a place in the grand prize judging. (Changed by Jaimie, 5 November 2026: every Graduate has a
   place in the grand prize judging, so nobody has to visit every place; the gold seal stays as an
   honour on the certificate. See docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-04-elf-paper-design.md, decision 15.)
9. **Grand prize:** judged, never drawn at random. Gold seal detectives (since 5 November 2026: Graduates) send a photo of their
   naughtiest elf at home, or a drawing. A judge NBCC chooses picks the winner on Christmas Eve,
   taking age into account. NBCC phones the grown-up and delivers on Christmas Day. (A paid entry
   plus a random draw is a lottery, and under-16s may not take part in a society lottery; a prize
   decided by judgement is a prize competition and needs no licence. Trustees read the Gambling
   Commission guidance before launch.)
10. **Paying:** pay what you can. £5 suggested per detective, any amount from £1; both set in the
    admin. The entry cannot have Gift Aid. A separate optional donation line can, and the usual
    cover-the-card-fee tick is offered. Adults can play too.
11. **Children's safety:** on the website a child appears only by detective name: first name plus a
    festive surname they pick ("Detective Ruby Tinselfoot"). Never a real surname, school or age. A
    photo goes public only if the grown-up says yes for that photo, and after a staff check, with the
    location data removed. All photos come down at the end of January, and children's details are
    deleted then.
12. **Calendar look:** a wooden board of 24 doors; before a door opens a shape carved in the wood
    hints at the prize. Staff upload each shape (one colour, transparent background); the website
    paints it as carved wood so all match; a door without a shape shows a mystery star. States:
    shut, today with a countdown, tomorrow, big prize, found.
13. **Badge:** an ID card on the family's page, to print or show on a phone: detective name, badge
    number, rank. It shows their photo (optional) or the elf they build, and appears on the "You're
    first!" screen.
14. **Wardrobe:** the elf's wardrobe grows as doors are done. Pieces (hats, glasses, scarves, tops,
    shoes, gadgets) come in several styles and colours, about three new things per door. Outfits are
    whole costumes applied in one tap. Every unlock is made obvious: a present opens on screen, new
    things carry a "New" tag until tried, and the next email shows their own elf in a new outfit.
    Locked pieces and outfits show faintly with a padlock.
15. **Outfits:** Workshop elf at sign-up; Present with a bow on its head at 3 doors; Christmas tree
    at 6; Christmas pudding at 9; Snowman at 12; Gingerbread elf at 15; Reindeer at 18; Highland
    piper at 21; Graduation robes at 24; Golden detective for the Gold seal; Nessie for winning a
    race.
16. **Asks:** only at proud moments. A warm thank-you with a gentle ask when they win an elf, are
    promoted or graduate, and an invite to fundraise at graduation. Daily emails carry only a small
    "give a little more" link. Money wording always uses "could". Share cards show the elf and rank,
    never a child's photo. The newsletter box at sign-up is unticked.
17. **Facts:** a four-week story for grown-ups in the daily email, with a short line for children on
    the door. The 24 facts and door lines were approved by Jaimie on 4 October and live in
    `src/elf/facts.ts`.
18. **Team prompts:** a morning "Elf HQ" email with the day's checklist and every social post ready
    (words and picture), plus an instant alert when an elf is found, with the winner's photo to
    approve. Recipients are set in the admin. Approving happens on a page with a button, never by
    following an emailed link alone.
19. **Prizes and map:** each place has a private prize list; a "Show off" tick features a prize
    publicly (Big prize ribbon and a teaser). An admin map based on the About us map shows a pin per
    place and shades anywhere more than about 10 miles from one.
20. **Agreed with the sign-off, to confirm when wiring up:** under-16s must have a grown-up with
    them at hiding places; a public map of the hiding places without their days; one elf per family;
    a name for each day's elf.

Still Jaimie's to choose: the grand prize and its judge.

## Rules, precisely

These are the rules the pure logic in `src/elf/` implements. Times are Europe/London.

- **Doors.** Door `n` (1 to 24) belongs to `n` December of the campaign year.
- **Clue time.** Each door has a clue time set by staff. Defaults: 17:30 on school days, 10:00 at
  weekends and on days staff mark as school holidays.
- **Door states.** `shut` (clue not out), `hunting` (clue out, elf not found), `found` (claimed),
  `revealed` (the place closed for the day with the elf unclaimed: the place is shown, the elf waits,
  and the first valid claim still wins). A door in `found` or `revealed` is "open".
- **Closing time.** Every door has one, so every door opens. A place with none set closes at 20:00.
  A closing time that is not after the clue means that time on the next day (a place open past
  midnight, or one that shut before its clue came out). No door's place is shown later than 20:00
  on Christmas Eve, so everyone can finish.
- **Guess.** One per family per door, chosen from places whose door is not open. Allowed only while
  the door is `hunting`. Changing it replaces it. When the door opens, a family whose guess was
  saved before it opened and matches the place has cracked it: every detective in the family at
  that moment gets the magnifying glass. Nothing says whether a guess is right until the door is
  open.
- **Claim.** Needs: a signed-in family, the door `hunting` or `revealed`, the secret word typed
  correctly (compared ignoring case, spaces, punctuation and accents), at least one detective
  chosen, and the family not having won an elf already. The first valid claim to arrive wins. The
  claim also counts as a visit for the detectives chosen.
- **Keeping the word secret.** Every poster stays up all month and is only a link, so anyone can
  try a word at any place from home. Three rules answer that. A wrong word gets the same answer at
  every place, whether or not an elf is hiding there that day, so trying a made up word everywhere
  finds nothing out. A family gets six wrong tries at a place. And a secret word has a letter and
  at least three numbers picked at random, like SNOWBALL 472, because the list of word ideas is
  public.
- **Stamp.** Needs: the door open, at least one detective chosen, and either a location within the
  allowed distance of the place (300 metres by default, never more than 2,000) or a photo that the
  server received. The phone's own estimate of its accuracy is not used: a phone that cannot get a
  good fix sends a photo. One stamp per detective per door. Both proofs are the family's word: they
  make a real visit the easy thing to do, they do not catch a determined cheat.
- **Progress.** Doors done = cracked or visited, counted once each. Rank from doors done: 0 Trainee,
  6 Junior Detective, 12 Detective, 18 Senior Detective, 24 Graduate. Gold seal = 24 visited.
- **Wardrobe.** A piece colour unlocks at a number of doors done. An outfit unlocks at its number of
  doors done, or for the Gold seal, or for a race win. A look is valid only if everything in it is
  unlocked for that detective.
- **Names.** A detective name is a first name plus a festive surname from the fixed list. The first
  name is one word of 1 to 20 characters in the letters A to Z, accents allowed, with at most one
  hyphen or apostrophe inside it. A space is refused, so a surname typed after the first name is
  never shown. It must pass the rude word checks however it is hyphenated, accented or dressed up in
  look alike letters.
- **Names in public.** A first name is shown in full only once a member of staff has ticked it.
  Until then the public sees its first letter: "Detective R. Tinselfoot". No list of words can stop
  every rude or revealing name, and a detective name goes on the calendar the moment an elf is
  found, so this is how "staff approve all public content" holds. A family always sees its own
  detectives' names in full. (Added after review. Jaimie chose it on 4 October 2026, over showing
  only the first letter for ever and over showing names straight away. It adds one tick for each
  new detective.)

## What the website already has that this reuses

Card payments with a donation line and fee cover (the Ball checkout), 6-digit sign-in codes
(fundraising), QR codes with scan counting, staff approval of photos (fundraiser news), print-ready
pages (business certificate), countdowns, picture processing with `sharp`, the blocked-word filter,
and the About us map.

Things that shape the build: emails go out at about 60 a minute, so the website is where the clue
appears first; the daily SES allowance is shared with receipts and sign-in codes and must be checked
before December; one app task and five database connections mean the live calendar polls a cheap,
cached feed; email clients do not show SVG, so each outfit picture is rendered to PNG ahead of time.

## Standing rules that apply

Staff approve all public content. Everything ships switched off and only an admin switches it on.
Recipient emails are set in the admin, never in code. No link in an email changes anything when
followed. No inner scrollbars; every screen works at phone width. The repo is public: tests use
invented data only. Money wording uses "could". Wording is plain English, with no dashes in public
copy.
