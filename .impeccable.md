# Impeccable design context — NBCC

Gathered from the client (Jaimie, NBCC) during the TASK-313 Festive Ball build, Aug 2026.
Not inferred from the codebase: every statement below traces to something the client said.

## Design Context

### Users

**Two audiences on one page, which is the central design problem of `/ball`.**

1. **People arriving from print and radio.** A glossy magazine advert lands 4 Sep 2026 carrying
   a QR code and "NBCC.SCOT". They have seen a black-and-gold poster, they want the party, and
   most of them are on a phone having just scanned a code. They need to recognise the page as
   the thing they were just looking at, within about four seconds.
2. **People who have never heard of NBCC.** The client's words: *"it might be the first time
   that somebody's hearing about us."* They are being asked for £100, or £1,000 for a table, by
   a charity they cannot place. They need the story before they will spend.

Serving one at the other's expense fails. The agreed structure repeats the call to action so
neither group has to scroll past the other's content.

### Brand personality

**Warm, plain-spoken, unpretentious.** A volunteer-run Ayrshire charity that delivered 7,657
Red Bags in 2025 and talks about "comfort, dignity and joy". It is not corporate, not slick,
and never solemn about itself.

The ball adds one note on top: **an elegant evening.** Black tie, gold foil, Michelle McManus.
The page has to hold both — the glamour of the night and the ordinariness of the cause — without
letting the glamour imply the charity spends money on itself.

Three words: **warm, direct, dignified.**

### Aesthetic direction

**NBCC brand throughout, with the poster's gold lockup at the top.** The client's decision,
verbatim: *"our branding but we'll use the title of their poster at the top"*, and separately
*"we'll provide the photo to add which is non-nbcc branding"*.

- **Light theme.** Correct for the context: this is browsed on a phone in daylight, on a
  commute, in a hairdresser's, from a magazine — not at night in a dark room. The site is
  cream-based and the ball page belongs to the site.
- **A dark hero band** carrying the supplied gold lockup, then the page settles into NBCC
  cream. The dark band is the handshake with the print advert; everything below is the charity.
- **Anti-reference:** it must not read as though NBCC is advertising a furniture retailer. The
  sponsor is credited generously but in its own band, clearly separate.

### Typography — a deliberate exception

The project's existing system wins over this skill's font-avoidance list. NBCC's brand kit is
**Playfair Display + Poppins**, self-hosted, already serving every page, and locked to a
2-font performance budget (REQ-005/REQ-033). Playfair appears on this skill's
reflex-fonts-to-reject list; it is used here anyway because it is *this organisation's actual
brand*, not a default reached for in the absence of one. Substituting a "more interesting" face
would make `/ball` look like a different website.

The gold lockup carries the display personality; the page type stays in the house faces.

### Design principles for this page

1. **Recognition first.** Someone holding the magazine must see the same thing on screen. The
   lockup is the artwork from the advert, not a re-typeset imitation.
2. **Real text over baked-in text.** The poster's supporting lines are unreadable at 375px and
   invisible to screen readers. Only the gold lockup ships as an image; the tagline, date,
   venue, price and everything else is live text.
3. **Repeat the ask.** Two calls to action, one before the story and one after, because the two
   audiences are convinced at different points.
4. **Honest scarcity only.** Remaining-ticket counts appear only below a threshold staff set,
   and are always true. Invented urgency breaches the Code of Fundraising Practice.
5. **Its own stylesheet.** `/ball` ships `assets/css/ball.css`, never additions to the shared
   bundle: donate.html sits 369 bytes under its enforced performance budget, and that is the
   donation money path.

### Accessibility

- The event is over-18s and the audience skews older than a typical web product: do not rely on
  small type or low-contrast gold-on-dark for anything that must be read.
- Venue access information (step-free, accessible toilets, hearing loop, blue-badge parking) is
  published on the page rather than hidden behind "contact us", so a disabled guest can decide
  to buy at 11pm without waiting on an email. This was an explicit client decision.
- Existing site conventions hold: skip link, visible focus, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected,
  every `<img>` with width/height and lazy loading.
